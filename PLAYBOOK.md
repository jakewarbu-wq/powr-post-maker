# Learn from the best: POWR content playbook

These are patterns that top Pilates studios and strong local studios use well. They are lessons, not templates. **Never copy another studio's wording, headline, layout or idea.** Take the principle and say it in POWR's own voice, about POWR's own classes and town.

Sources (October 2026): articles on studios with strong Instagram marketing (Sunset Beach Pilates, Pilates with Adrien, Authentic Pilates Learning Center, Good Day Pilates, Sunshine Pilates), guides to Pilates and fitness studio content, UK reformer studio round-ups, and the websites of studios near Clitheroe. Instagram itself can't be read automatically, so this is based on those, not on scraped posts.

## What the best do

1. **Hook in the first line.** The first line is all most people see. Lead with a question, a surprising fact or a feeling people recognise ("Stiff after a day at the desk?"), never with "Hi everyone" or the studio name.
2. **One idea per post.** Hook, one useful thing, one call to action. Posts that try to say three things get skimmed.
3. **Teach, so people save it.** Saved posts travel furthest. Good formats: one move explained (how to set up, the common mistake, the fix), myth vs reality, "3 signs you'd love Reformer", a posture check people can do at home.
4. **Take the fear out of the first class.** The biggest barrier is nerves. Answer real beginner questions: what to wear, what happens in the first class, "you don't need to be flexible", "lots of people come on their own".
5. **Sense of place.** The best studios feel rooted in their town. Mention the Ribble Valley seasons, a walk up to the castle, the York Street studio itself, the cosy feel in winter.
6. **Show the people and the room, honestly.** Familiar, warm and real beats glossy. (POWR's auto posts have no photos, so the words carry the warmth; suggest in the caption that Caroline adds a real class photo or a studio corner through "Use my own photo instead" when she can.)
7. **Values, not just timetables.** Say what POWR stands for: strength for everyday life, feeling good in your body, a calm welcoming space. The name is POWR, so lean into strength.
8. **One clear call to action.** One ask per post: book via the link in bio, save this, send it to a friend, or reply with a word. Never more than one.
9. **Make it easy to reply.** Ask a simple question people can answer in one word, or "comment READY and we'll send you the intro offer details" (only when the offer exists in the calendar).
10. **Use the price when it helps.** Studios that show prices plainly win trust. POWR's single Reformer class (£18) and 3-class Reformer Foundations (£45) are good value: state them simply, without knocking anyone.
11. **Series people recognise.** A repeating slot builds habit: "Wednesday tip", "Pilates for runners / desk workers / new mums / over-50s", "Saturday stretch".
12. **Stories do the selling.** In the Stories steps, suggest a poll ("Morning or evening class?"), a question box, or the link sticker to book.

## What local studios do well that POWR can do its own way

- A founder face and a personal note builds trust. POWR could share Caroline's own reasons for opening the studio (only with her words; never invent them).
- Plain, visible starter offers. POWR's £45 intro is strong; mention it in value posts.
- Reassurance for nervous beginners ("many come alone", "no experience needed").
- Reviews shown as quote cards. POWR should only use real reviews Caroline gives us, with permission; never invent one.
- "Strength for life" messaging. POWR owns this naturally through its name.

Never name another studio in a post, and never compare POWR to a named business.

## Avoid

- Captions that just describe the image or say "Come to class!".
- Vague motivation with nothing useful in it.
- Hard selling, countdowns or pressure ("last chance!") unless the calendar has a real deadline.
- Several calls to action in one caption.
- Overproduced or jargon-heavy language. Keep it warm and plain.
- Anything about Barre (on hold).
